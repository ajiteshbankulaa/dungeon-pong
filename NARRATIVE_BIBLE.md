# The Cathedral of Return — narrative bible

## Promise and length

A 20–30 hour melancholic adventure about grief becoming memory instead of a
prison. The emotional rhythm moves from loneliness to wonder, fellowship,
suspicion, righteous anger, horror, forgiveness, and brilliant earned joy.
Comedy and small domestic details keep the sadness human rather than oppressive.

Implemented structure: five dungeons of 25 rooms, 125 named combat rooms total,
roughly 16–20 campaign hours plus 6–10 hours of optional character rooms,
rematches, alternate routes, and endings. Combat rooms average 8–12 minutes;
story, puzzle, rest, shop, and exploration rooms create pacing.

## The buried truth

The player is told the Keeper is a fragment of Ilyan, a child who died while
playing homemade Pong with his older sister Mara. That is false. Mara died
saving Ilyan during a hospital electrical fire. The adult Ilyan built the
Cathedral to preserve one final match, then split himself in two:

- the Architect holds his terrified need to keep the loop unchanged;
- the Keeper holds the courage and tenderness he associated with Mara.

The monsters are not corruptions. They are Ilyan's contradictory original
memories resisting replacement by cleaner copies. Every early victory really
does erase something. The twist must feel surprising on first play and
inevitable on replay: pronouns are avoided in old records, two shadows appear
in reflections, the serve always originates from Ilyan's side, and Mara's
supposed memories contain details only the survivor could know.

## Cast

- **The Keeper / Mara:** the playable consciousness. Direct, guarded, and
  increasingly funny; becomes a person through choices rather than provenance.
- **Moss:** a slime made from the green hospital night-light. Comic, devoted,
  frightened of being considered disposable. Emotional heart of the party.
- **The Hunter / June:** Ilyan's memory of physical therapy and learning aim.
  Starts severe, becomes exuberantly adventurous.
- **Phantom Queen / Ada:** abandoned possible futures. Speaks grandly to hide
  loneliness; chooses companionship over certainty.
- **Bullet Saint:** guilt given discipline. Refuses easy forgiveness but teaches
  repair.
- **Angel and Lord 666:** the false moral binary Ilyan used to sort grief into
  "good" sadness and "bad" anger. Their reconciliation is loud and funny.
- **The Architect / Ilyan:** antagonist and wounded survivor, never a secret
  supervillain. The final victory is persuading him to tolerate tomorrow.

## Five-act room plan

### I. The Silt Arcade — grief and wonder (20 rooms)

The Keeper wakes alone, meets Moss, and restores a drowned arcade. Tutorial
rooms teach movement, angled returns, telegraphs, banks, and mercy choices.
Optional rooms contain tiny happy memories: burnt toast, a ruler paddle, a joke
with no punchline. Bone King guards names the system marked for deletion.

Climax: the player chooses to preserve his memorial even though doing so makes
the boss mechanically harder. The dungeon ends adventurous and hopeful, with
the first outdoor-looking sky—later revealed as a hospital ceiling.

### II. The Hunt Gallery — friendship and exhilaration (20 rooms)

June hunts the party through moving maze corridors. Routes loop and create
shortcuts; the player learns projectile reflection, dual-ball routing, and
environmental sight lines. Ada's phantoms show choices the loop rejected.

Climax: recruit June and Ada or defeat them cleanly. Both survive, but later
dialogue and the final assist differ. This act supplies speed, jokes, rivalry,
and the campaign's most openly adventurous stretch.

### III. The Dream Engine — suspicion and horror (22 rooms)

Gravity rooms descend through sleeping memory stacks. Victories create visible
gaps in music and scenery. The Chorister reveals replacement, not restoration.
The party becomes angry with the Keeper; Moss briefly leaves. Optional rescue
rooms can preserve erased NPC fragments at the cost of harder combat routes.

Climax: Bullet Saint does not forgive the player. It asks for restitution.
Stopping the Engine proves impossible while its creator still needs the loop.

### IV. The Broken Heavens — anger and truth (20 rooms)

Zeus, Angel, and Lord 666 wage a theatrical war over who deserves memory.
Courts split, reverse, collapse, and reconnect like a low-key dungeon maze.
The party learns the hospital story and believes the Keeper is dead Ilyan.
Moss returns by choice, arguing that origin does not cancel personhood.

Climax: Angel reveals the first half of the twist—the Keeper carries Mara, not
Ilyan. The party rejects the heaven/hell binary and opens a third route.

### V. The Infinite Court — revelation and joy (18 rooms)

Rooms remix earlier mechanics without erasing their identity. Recovered NPCs
physically alter routes. The full twist lands: Ilyan survived; the Keeper and
Architect are his divided mind using Mara as a shape for courage and fear.

The final match changes from duel to cooperative rally. Winning by damage earns
the Loop ending. Preserving every available memory unlocks Return: all paddles
keep one impossible rally alive while the Cathedral dissolves into sunrise.
The epilogue follows adult Ilyan through breakfast, therapy, friendship, and a
small community arcade named Mara's Return. Happiness is brilliant because
loss remains true, not because it is undone.

## Ending states

1. **Loop:** accept preservation; safe, beautiful, quietly devastating.
2. **Break:** destroy the Cathedral; honest but incomplete release.
3. **Return:** integrate Keeper and Architect, export the inhabitants as new
   creative work, and live forward. Requires memory rescues and relationships.

## Writing rules

- Never explain emotion twice; use objects, interrupted jokes, and changed
  behavior.
- Every major character gets desire, fear, comic texture, and a choice unrelated
  to serving the protagonist.
- Boss dialogue responds to play style, retries, perfect clears, and mercy.
- Sadness never equals importance. Wonder, anger, and joy receive equal craft.
- Seed the twist in every dungeon and reveal it in three escalating turns.
