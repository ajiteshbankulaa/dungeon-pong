# What Returns: grief, return, and the cup

Narrative architecture revision — 3 November 2026.

## Status

Implemented now: removed the coda's ticket errand and rocking table. Player places one cup at the table, sees a line about reaching for the missing second cup, then picks up their own. The old cup ring remains. Existing replay/progression preserved.

The expanded score below is a proposed next narrative/mechanics pass, not a claim that these interactions are already implemented. The current game still skips standalone morning Angle and Between; these should become meaningful room actions rather than adding two more empty shots.

## Scientific foundation and limits

Do not call the rooms stages of grief or claim to cover every emotion. Grief differs between people and is not a universal sequence. Stroebe, Schut and Boerner's critique of stage models is explicit about the lack of evidence for a prescribed order: https://doi.org/10.1177/0030222817691870

Use oscillation as the structural principle: attention moves between the loss and the tasks of living, with respite as well as pain. This is an artistic application of the dual-process model, not a simulation or treatment: https://doi.org/10.1080/074811899201046

A relationship can change after bereavement without being erased. Continuing bonds can involve comfort as well as distress; keeping a bond is not automatically healthy or unhealthy. Do not make destroying the machine, forgetting, or discarding a keepsake a victory requirement. Review: https://doi.org/10.1080/07481187.2023.2223593

## Literary foundation

Dickinson's 'After great pain, a formal feeling comes' locates pain in automatic bodily action and altered time. Apply that through the paddle preparing for a return that cannot arrive, not through diagnostic text. https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poems/47651/after-great-pain-a-formal-feeling-comes-372

Bishop's 'One Art' makes an attempted assertion of control increasingly difficult to sustain. Apply that through repeated serves whose confident promise cracks. This is a reading of the poem's craft, not an empirical claim about grief. https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poems/47536/one-art

Keep three physical anchors: the shared return, the empty hook, and the cup ring. Retire the ticket as a narrative prop. A new metaphor must have a visible object/action and repay its introduction later.

## Proposed fifteen-beat score

The arrangement is a dramatic progression with emotional reversals, not a clinical timeline. Five to ten minutes depending on reading and retries; no forced reading deadlines.

### Tuesday: teach the body something it will keep doing

1. Serve — Trust. Space serves; generous return, recognizable cuff lift. Opening: 'You always let me serve first.' Closing: 'You sent it back / where I could reach.' No premature grief foreshadowing in the art.

2. Angle — Attention and play. One corner shot. Opening: 'Show me how you do that.' Closing: 'You missed the ball. / Again. I was watching your hand.' Let a small sound of amusement accompany the miss; do not label it romance or gender the companion.

3. Between — Reciprocal care. Two comfortable placements within their receiving band. Opening: 'We stopped trying to catch each other out.' Closing: 'Just far enough to make you reach. / Not so far you had to leave.' This is the memory's central physical grammar: both people make continuation possible.

4. Hold — Ordinary pleasure. Four exchanges form a small complete melody. Opening: 'The last bus stopped outside.' Closing: 'You could have gone. / Still, you stayed for another exchange.' A bus stop light visible through the window makes the line legible. If we cannot show that physical context, replace the bus rather than explaining it in another paragraph.

5. Again? — Unfinished ordinary time. One final crossing. Opening: 'One more? / You were already taking off your coat.' Closing: 'I thought we had all the evenings.' Night street fragment, silence, one coat and an empty hook. Do not imply a causal chain of staying to play causing the accident. Do not show a hit or assign fault.

### Reconstruction: knowing is different from being able to stop wanting

6. Serve — Yearning with brief relief. First exact replay includes the cuff lift and original interval. Opening: 'For a moment, I knew what came next.' Closing: 'Your cuff. The small lift. / My hand answered.' Let recognition feel genuinely good before confronting absence. The game should not scold the player for returning.

7. Angle — Control, frustration, and an impossible bargain. Player chooses the saved angle; a forgiving tolerance accepts it. Opening: 'This time, I put it exactly where you wanted.' Closing: 'The shot changed. / The evening did not.' Exactness offers competence, not a resurrection. Optional retries may change the shot; no mandatory failure and no failure-count gate.

8. Between — Anger without a new mechanic. Comfortable return has no visible effort on the other side: the saved paddle meets it mechanically. Opening: 'Miss it. Just once.' Closing: 'It caught every ball. / That was not what I meant.' The wish is for a person to surprise them, not for the software to become difficult. Preserve the generous task and make its success emotionally inadequate.

9. Hold — Tenderness returning; fear of losing detail. Two tones, blue-green answered, purple crossing independently. Opening: 'Was your laugh before the miss, / or after?' Closing: 'I remembered the way it interrupted mine.' Give the familiar melodic fragment one clean, warm appearance inside the mechanical score. Memory must retain value even when inaccurate. Do not add a memory meter.

10. Again? — Exhaustion and a reversible boundary. Opening: 'One more.' Closing: 'I had stopped playing. / I was keeping the evening from ending.' Offer replay or step away; both clearly signposted. A replay is allowed and never mocked. On stepping away: 'I wanted to hear you again. / I also needed the room to be quiet.' This is a decision about this moment, not a cure or rejection of the companion.

### Morning: ordinary life and loss can occupy the same room

11. Serve — Numb routine; body ahead of knowledge. One finger-drawn shot, empty right court. Opening: 'Morning. / I did not open the curtains.' Closing: 'The light came in anyway.' Daylight is ordinary and subdued, not a victory wash or spiritual transcendence.

12. Angle — A moment of remembered pleasure. Aim a trace toward the old corner, no target or failure. Opening: 'That corner.' Closing: 'I almost heard you say it.' A tiny answer from the Tuesday motif, played acoustically. The loss need not dominate every second.

13. Between — Daily action interrupted by habit. Replace footprint obstacle with approaching the actual table/cup ring. Opening: 'I reached for two.' Player places one cup; stop the reaching gesture visibly before a second appears. Closing: 'My hand knew the shelf / better than I knew the morning.' No phantom person or materializing second cup.

14. Hold — A wave returns. Empty-court shot, 1.5 seconds of still-active paddle movement. Opening: 'Your side is open.' Closing: 'My hand was ready / before I remembered.' This happens AFTER a warm memory and useful ordinary task. It proves that doing something for oneself did not finish grief.

15. Again? — Continuing bond without demanding an answer. Allow one last trace or leave the ball at rest. Closing: 'I will want another game. / Not this morning.' Avoid a moralized good/bad ending. A remembered cuff gesture can survive in the player's own hand movement, not in a resurrected opponent.

## Coda: care, not a symbolic disposal puzzle

Current focused implementation:
- Walk to the table and place one cup. Ceramic contact.
- Text: 'I set out one cup. / My hand went back for yours.'
- E at the same table picks up the cup. No errand back to the machine.
- Text: 'Then I picked up mine. / It was still warm.'
- Show the cup held by the player and the companion's old cup ring on the table together.
- One final piano chord, title. No declaration that the player has accepted death.

For the larger pass, move the first placement into morning Between and retain the pickup as coda. Do not force the player to place the cup twice. Let warmth mean the living person's present physical need, rather than promise that grief is over.

## Presentation and verification for the larger pass

Warmth must return briefly inside reconstruction; morning must still contain shadow and loss. Do not map one emotion to one color and mechanically complete it. The three score movements can share one interval but change who answers it. End without a triumphant cadence.

Player input must carry the transition: serve, adjust, try the familiar placement, replay or step away, reach, prepare for an absent return, pick up the cup. Never display names of emotions as objectives. Avoid forced losing, punished replay, input inversion, hidden Enter prompts, and compulsory waiting except the brief rupture/unanswered beat.

Playtest questions: Can someone describe who the figures were to each other without the game assigning an identity? Did a replay feel comforting before it felt insufficient? Did they expect a return during morning Hold? Do they read the cup pickup as continuing today, rather than discarding the companion? Can they explain why they left the arcade machine? Do they feel allowed to return another day?
