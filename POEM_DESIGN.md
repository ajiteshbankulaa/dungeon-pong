# WHAT RETURNS — revised story and interaction plan

Status: proposed short-game design, not implemented behavior. This document supersedes the named-character, long-campaign direction in NARRATIVE_BIBLE.md for the poem adaptation. See POEM_ARCHITECTURE.md for implementation.

## Emotional foundation

A 5–10 minute hybrid of side-view character movement and Pong. Three loops revisit the same five rooms: Serve, Angle, Between, Hold, Again. The specific feeling is fearing that allowing grief to soften means abandoning someone who died.

Use “I” and “you” in the poem. No visible proper names, fixed genders, or specified family/romantic relationship. Internal technical IDs are player and other; never expose those as character titles. Distinguish the two people through silhouette, posture, gestures, and ordinary shared actions. Self-insertion comes from an open relationship and recognizable behavior, not featureless characters.

The underlying story is clear: two people played together; one died; the survivor reconstructs the evening; repetition becomes compulsion; the survivor learns to revisit a memory without demanding it become present. The exact nature of the final opposing paddle remains open. Samsara, attachment, emptiness, and release inform the structure; avoid claiming grief follows five universal stages or equating acceptance with forgetting.

## Modes and transitions

MEMORY: one small side-view room, horizontal movement with A/D or left/right, E interaction. Window, table, and cabinet are the main anchors. No jumping, combat, inventory, puzzles involving obscure triggers, or failure. Mandatory walks take approximately 2–4 seconds. Most interludes require one action and last 5–12 seconds.

PONG: W/S or up/down, Space serve; paddle contact offset determines angle. Short explicit objectives, forgiving retries, no HP, abilities, moral score, or punitive restarts. Targets widen after repeated attempts. Completed exchange progress survives misses.

POEM: action-triggered fragments displayed in safe pauses. E/Enter advances; first press can reveal full text. Never require reading a paragraph during a live rally.

Characters align with paddle positions before silhouettes crossfade into the court. The table becomes an obstacle; the window frame becomes the arena. Returning to character mode restores spatial continuity. Cup contact echoes paddle audio. These bridges make the modes parts of one memory.

## Minute-by-minute sequence

Times are targets, not enforced waits. Reading holds may extend them. The first two rooms preserve the user's requested opening closely.

### 0:00–0:35 — loop 1 / Serve

Dark screen. Two paddles fade into warm yellowish light. “You always let me serve first.” Ball rests near player. Prompt: “Space: serve · W/S or arrows: move.” Return three incoming balls. Opponent aims generously toward player and makes a distinctive small upward movement before a low shot.

After completion, paddles resolve into two human figures at a cabinet. One turns toward the other; the player takes a few steps and E interacts. A small tap on the cabinet invites another game. Establish two people through animation without explanatory labels.

### 0:35–1:10 — loop 1 / Angle

Hit a small target behind opponent using an edge return. Opponent has a visibly limited recovery window so the shot is achievable. Missing costs nothing; ball returns. Widen target after three unsuccessful rallies. Success pauses play: “You only know one trick.” / “It worked on you.” Other character gives a restrained shoulder laugh.

Meaning: teasing, habits, ordinary pleasure. Give the player something worth missing before introducing loss.

### 1:10–1:40 — loop 1 / Between

Memory: walk to crooked table, E shifts it while the other person holds the far end. “We moved the table / instead of stopping.” Table transforms into obstacle. Bank around it to hit two generous marks, revealing two cups and rain at a window. Meaning: the relationship inhabits a specific, imperfect place.

### 1:40–2:10 — loop 1 / Hold

Memory: other person places a cup beside player, who approaches cabinet to continue. “We played until / the window held more of us / than the room.” Pong: four shared exchanges, both paddles contributing. Each adds a note; misses retain progress. Meaning: sustaining a rally is companionship.

### 2:10–2:45 — loop 1 / Again

Play one final point; either scorer completes it. Opponent offers a readable opening after several exchanges. “That was the last game / we played together.” Score holds. Characters reappear. Warm room fades into a sparse bedside composition.

### 2:45–3:10 — the loss, once

An authored, noninteractive 12–20 second held-pose scene: other person lies still in bed; survivor sits holding their hand. Muted red enters as a steady bedside light and restrained accent. A time dissolve leaves an empty bed and the survivor's hand in the same position. Plain factual anchor: “You died that winter.” Then: “For a while, / my hand kept its shape.” No blood, graphic cause, flashing wash, heartbeat minigame, or implication that a missed Pong shot caused death.

Fade to survivor alone at cabinet. Walk to it and E activates replay. “I built something / that could answer again.” Rewind bridge. The cause of death stays unspecified. A skip option preserves the factual line and resumes at cabinet.

### 3:10–3:35 — loop 2 / Serve

Same court, one note missing. Return three delayed but readable shots. Signature gesture repeats twice. “You always let me— / Sometimes I asked.” Human bridge: a faint figure repeats the same turn; survivor interacts with cabinet. Meaning: recollection is already being edited.

### 3:35–4:00 — loop 2 / Angle

Target alternates between two telegraphed positions. Hit once. “I corrected your hesitation. / Then I missed it.” Gesture becomes mechanically exact. Meaning: making a reconstruction reliable erases its individuality.

### 4:00–4:35 — loop 2 / Between

Memory: a cup is missing. E on its visible outline restores it briefly. “The cups were blue. / One of them.” Pong: bank through two fragments to restore details temporarily. First restoration dims as the next appears. “I remember remembering.” Never promise a preserve-everything objective.

### 4:35–5:10 — loop 2 / Hold

Two staggered balls, one supporting lamplight, one rain. Sustain either through a 20-second sequence; dropped balls re-enter after a readable delay. Machine-driven fading concludes regardless of performance. “While I remembered your hands / I forgot what we were laughing at.” Meaning: preservation becomes divided attention and exhausting maintenance.

### 5:10–5:45 — loop 2 / Again

Opponent repeats opening-loop movement motif. Either point completes room. Teasing line repeats with identical timing; survivor answers “I know.” Score resets without input. Memory: faint figure walks table-to-cabinet twice. Cabinet begins serving by itself. “It no longer needed me / to begin.” Player walks toward clearly illuminated window threshold to interrupt repetition. No hidden inactivity puzzle.

### 5:45–6:15 — loop 3 / Serve

Memory: survivor at window. Daylight enters independently of movement. “For a while / I did not play. / The morning happened anyway.” Approach cabinet when ready. Space serves. One wall return, then faint opponent answers the next two. Meaning: beginning becomes a choice again.

### 6:15–6:45 — loop 3 / Angle

Aim two shots into a clearly marked reachable region around opponent. Opponent reciprocates toward player. Scoring past them does not erase progress. “I made a shot / you could return.” Brief memory bridge: faint figure sits rather than repeating its old walk. Meaning: attention becomes accommodation instead of control.

### 6:45–7:15 — loop 3 / Between

Memory: walk through space where table stood. No restoration interaction. “There is a space / where the lamp was. / Light reaches it.” Pong: send one shot through newly open lane. Absence changes actual geometry. Essential boundaries stay visible.

### 7:15–8:00 — loop 3 / Hold

Four shared exchanges; opponent has visible limited reach with generous recovery. Misses preserve progress. After a miss, signature upward gesture: “Again?” No rewind. Completion: “We missed things / even then.” Human bridge: two figures at cabinet, no longer perfectly aligned with paddles. Meaning: an imperfect exchange can continue without reconstruction.

### 8:00–8:40 — loop 3 / Again

“I cannot remember / whether you won.” Play one point; either outcome leads to same ending. Score remains. “I was there.” Allow movement for 2–3 seconds, then transition. No secret ending input; no moral reward for deliberately losing. Meaning: an event can remain past without becoming meaningless.

### 8:40–9:10 — coda

Survivor in ordinary room. Walk to table; E puts down one cup. Sound echoes paddle contact. “This morning / I could not remember / the sound of your laugh.” Bird outside slips and recovers; small shoulder-laugh animation. “Something was funny. / I laughed.” Daylight is muted, not triumphant. Title: WHAT RETURNS. Replay opening becomes “I remember you letting me serve first.” Replaying acknowledges memory rather than reversing the ending.

## Art and animation direction

Clean, softly illustrated side-view figures; controlled contours, limited interior detail, subtle static texture, generous negative space. Mostly charcoal #15171B and ivory #E8E5DC. Warm yellow #D9C58F supports shared warmth; desaturated blue #9AAEBE supports distance/daylight. Muted red #A65C63 belongs to the loss scene, never failure feedback. Palette values are starting points subject to contrast checks.

Make the two figures human and distinct without prescribing identity: different sleeve shapes, stance, head angle, stride, and gestural habits. Neutral clothing; avoid mandatory age/gender cues. The survivor may subtly change posture between loops rather than becoming a named adult/child pair. Test readability at 80–110 logical pixels high within 1100x680.

Minimum sprite clips: survivor idle, 4-frame walk, reach, sit, small laugh; other idle, turn, 4-frame walk, cup placement, laugh, seated and still bedside poses. Mirror walks where useful. Use held poses and dissolves for loss. Reuse one room background with stateful object layers and one sparse bedside composition. No fifteen bespoke environments.

Essential ball, prompts, targets, and court boundaries never fade with memory art. Reduced motion substitutes dissolves for rewinds. Text is high contrast, advanceable, and readable without color cues. The three-note motif loses layers in loop 2 and returns more simply in loop 3.

## Narrative acceptance

Players recognize two people before loss, understand someone died and the game is reconstruction, notice a changed recurring detail, and can explain the current action in both modes. Missing a shot never means failed love. The story remains open about the relationship and final paddle, while the practical events remain comprehensible.
